    The two token-event extractors disagree (Rule 2, S5 slice 2) #

    S5(←) (EVENTSOF-CONCEPT §9) was to connect TokenStateOk ts := SerializationWellFormed (tokenEventsOf ts) — TokenGrammar.lean's own extractor, the premise of parseStream_accepts — to the serialization layer's checkFrom {} (tokenEvents ts) (S2's TokenEvents.tokenEvents), ideally by the equality tokenEventsOf ts = tokenEvents ts. The Rule-2 grid refutes that equality, on scanner output and on hand-built arrays, so this file pins the gap instead of a proof around it (capstone 7.7 parse_iff_grammar via S5; the connecting lemma is blocked on the owner's ruling).

    The seam is the %TAG handle rule:

    • tokEvents declares every %TAG handle that is not a builtin (¬ BuiltinTagHandle h), immediately, and begins the document at the first directive;
    • tokenEvents declares only isNamedHandle handles (!word!) and flushes them at the ---, never if no --- follows.

    The parser registers every %TAG handle prepareDocumentState sees (ParseState.tagHandles), whether or not a --- follows — the tokEvents reading. Four shapes:

    1. "%TAG !h tag:x,2000:\n--- !h x" — was scanner-reachable until the [89] fix (now invalidTagHandle). The scanner copies the %TAG handle !h without checking its closing !, the parser registers !h, tokEvents declares it, tokenEvents does not. Both checkers accept (!h x scans as the non-specific tag ! plus a scalar, so the handle is never used), but the traces differ: [beginDocument, declareTag "!h", useTag "!"] vs [beginDocument, useTag "!"].
    2. A %TAG !h whose handle IS used (.tag "!h" … — not scanner-reachable): parser ok, the retired checkTokenState true, checkFrom {} (tokenEvents ts) false.
    3. A directive run with no --- (not scanner-reachable, directiveWithoutDocument): the parser and tokEvents begin the document at the directive and register the handle; tokenEvents keeps it pending until --- (never) or end of input.
    4. %TAG !a_b! (malformed named handle, scanner-refused): ¬ BuiltinTagHandle but not isNamedHandle — declared by one extractor, not the other.

    What DOES hold on the grid — on the listed rows only (agreeing below), no claim is made beyond them: the two extractors agree on each of these scanner-shaped inputs, whose %TAG handles are builtin or !word! and whose directive runs end at --- — %TAG before ---, two %TAGs, a duplicated %TAG, a handle redeclared after ..., %YAML + %TAG in either order (pinned below as the strongest true statement the grid supports).
